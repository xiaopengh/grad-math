\begin{exercise}[Deterministic time-dependent volatility]
\textbf{Restatement.}
Price a European call when
\(\dd S_t/S_t=\mu\dd t+\sigma_t\dd B_t\), where \(\sigma_t\) is deterministic,
positive, and square-integrable.  Compute \(\Delta\) and \(\Theta\), and compare
with the constant-volatility BMS surface.

\begin{solution}
Set
\[
  I_t=\int_t^T\sigma_s^2\,\dd s,\qquad \Sigma_t=\sqrt{I_t},
  \qquad \tau=T-t.
\]
Since \(\sigma_t>0\), the market price of risk is the deterministic function
\(\lambda_t=(\mu-r)/\sigma_t\).  The Girsanov density is
\[
  \frac{\dd\Q}{\dd\P}\bigg|_{\F_T}
    =\exp\left(
       -\int_0^T\lambda_s\,\dd B_s
       -\frac12\int_0^T\lambda_s^2\,\dd s
     \right),
\]
and \(B_t^\Q=B_t+\int_0^t\lambda_s\,\dd s\) is Brownian under \(\Q\).  Thus
\[
  \frac{\dd S_t}{S_t}=r\,\dd t+\sigma_t\,\dd B_t^\Q.
\]
Conditionally on \(S_t=x\),
\[
  S_T=x\exp\left(r\tau-\frac12I_t+\int_t^T\sigma_s\,\dd B_s^\Q\right),
\]
and the stochastic integral is Gaussian with mean \(0\) and variance \(I_t\).
Consequently
\[
  u(t,x)=x\normalcdf(d_1(t,x))-K\e^{-r\tau}\normalcdf(d_2(t,x)),
\]
where
\[
  d_1(t,x)=\frac{\log(x/K)+r\tau+\frac12 I_t}{\Sigma_t},
  \qquad
  d_2(t,x)=d_1(t,x)-\Sigma_t.
\]

At \(t=0\), this is exactly the ordinary BMS price with constant volatility
\[
  \bar\sigma=\sqrt{\frac1T\int_0^T\sigma_s^2\,\dd s},
\]
because the total variance to maturity is
\(\bar\sigma^2T=I_0\).

As in the constant-volatility case,
\[
  \Delta(t,x)=\partial_xu(t,x)=\normalcdf(d_1(t,x)),
\]
and
\[
  \Gamma(t,x)=\frac{\normalpdf(d_1(t,x))}{x\Sigma_t}.
\]
The time-dependent BMS PDE is
\[
  \partial_tu+rx\partial_xu+\frac12\sigma_t^2x^2\partial_{xx}u=ru.
\]
Therefore
\[
  \Theta(t,x)=\partial_tu(t,x)
    =-rK\e^{-r\tau}\normalcdf(d_2(t,x))
     -\frac{\sigma_t^2x\normalpdf(d_1(t,x))}{2\Sigma_t}.
\]

Suppose that the whole price surface on \([0,T]\) were the price surface of a
constant-volatility model with some volatility \(\widehat\sigma\).  Then the
total remaining variance would have to agree for every \(t\):
\[
  \widehat\sigma^2(T-t)=\int_t^T\sigma_s^2\,\dd s.
\]
Differentiating in \(t\) gives \(\widehat\sigma^2=\sigma_t^2\) for almost every
\(t\).  Hence the only way a constant-volatility surface can match for all
dates is that \(\sigma_t\) is constant almost everywhere.  If \(\sigma_t\) is not
constant, the match at \(t=0\) does not extend to the whole interval.
\end{solution}
\end{exercise}
