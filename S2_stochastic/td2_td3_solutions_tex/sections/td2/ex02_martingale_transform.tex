\begin{exercise}[Martingale transform]
\emph{Restatement.}  Let \(M\) be an \(\F\)-martingale and let
\((\Delta_n)_{n\ge 0}\) be bounded and adapted.  Prove that
\[
  X_0=0,\qquad
  X_n=\sum_{k=1}^n \Delta_{k-1}(M_k-M_{k-1})
\]
is an \(\F\)-martingale.
\end{exercise}

\begin{solution}
This is the discrete version of a stochastic integral with predictable
integrand.  Since \(\Delta\) is adapted, \(\Delta_{k-1}\) is
\(\F_{k-1}\)-measurable before the martingale increment
\(M_k-M_{k-1}\) is observed.  Since \(\Delta\) is bounded, say
\(|\Delta_n|\le c\) a.s., each summand is integrable:
\[
  \E\bigl[|\Delta_{k-1}(M_k-M_{k-1})|\bigr]
    \le c\bigl(\E[|M_k|]+\E[|M_{k-1}|]\bigr)<\infty .
\]
Thus \(X_n\) is integrable.  It is also \(\F_n\)-measurable, because every
term in the sum up to \(n\) is \(\F_n\)-measurable.

For the martingale property, write
\[
  X_{n+1}=X_n+\Delta_n(M_{n+1}-M_n).
\]
Here \(X_n\) and \(\Delta_n\) are \(\F_n\)-measurable.  Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \E[X_{n+1}\mid \F_n]
    &=X_n+\Delta_n\E[M_{n+1}-M_n\mid \F_n]  \\
    &=X_n+\Delta_n\bigl(\E[M_{n+1}\mid \F_n]-M_n\bigr) \\
    &=X_n.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last equality is exactly the martingale property of \(M\), as in
\polyref{Def.~A.6}.  Hence \(X\) is an \(\F\)-martingale.
\end{solution}
