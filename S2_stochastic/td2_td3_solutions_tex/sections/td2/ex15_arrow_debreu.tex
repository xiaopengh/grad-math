\begin{exercise}[Arrow--Debreu prices and the risk-neutral probability]
In a complete discrete-time market with one risky asset and a risk-neutral
probability, assume \(S^1_{t_k}\) takes finitely many values.  Compare the
prices of Arrow--Debreu claims with the risk-neutral probabilities, and
generalize to \(d>1\).
\end{exercise}

\begin{solution}
Let the possible terminal values of the risky asset be
\[
  s_1,\ldots,s_M.
\]
For each \(j\), define the Arrow--Debreu payoff
\[
  G^j=\1_{\{S^1_T=s_j\}}.
\]
Because the market is complete, \(G^j\) is replicable.  By the risk-neutral
pricing formula for a replicable contingent claim \(\polyref{Prop.~1.7}\),
\[
  p^{G^j}_{t_k}
  =
  e^{-r(T-t_k)}
  \EQ\!\left[\1_{\{S^1_T=s_j\}}\given\F_{t_k}\right]
  =
  e^{-r(T-t_k)}
  \Q\!\left(S^1_T=s_j\given\F_{t_k}\right).
\]
Equivalently, the \(T\)-capitalized price is the conditional risk-neutral
probability:
\[
  e^{r(T-t_k)}p^{G^j}_{t_k}
  =
  \Q\!\left(S^1_T=s_j\given\F_{t_k}\right).
\]
At the initial date, when \(\F_{t_0}\) is trivial, this becomes
\[
  p^{G^j}_{t_0}
  =
  e^{-rT}\Q(S^1_T=s_j).
\]
Thus Arrow--Debreu prices are exactly discounted state probabilities under
\(\Q\).  Knowing all Arrow--Debreu prices is the same as knowing the
risk-neutral law of \(S^1_T\).

For \(d>1\), let the vector of risky terminal prices take values
\[
  z_\ell=(z_\ell^1,\ldots,z_\ell^d),\qquad \ell=1,\ldots,L.
\]
The Arrow--Debreu payoff for state \(z_\ell\) is
\[
  G^\ell=\1_{\{(S^1_T,\ldots,S^d_T)=z_\ell\}}.
\]
The same argument gives
\[
  p^{G^\ell}_{t_k}
  =
  e^{-r(T-t_k)}
  \Q\!\left((S^1_T,\ldots,S^d_T)=z_\ell\given\F_{t_k}\right).
\]
Consequently the family of Arrow--Debreu prices recovers the full joint
risk-neutral distribution of the terminal risky-asset vector.  This is the
finite-state version of the replication and valuation principle developed in
\(\polyref{Sec.~1.4}\).
\end{solution}
